Why I do this

I know what it feels like to not have a home.

I started from zero in this country. Before real estate, there were basement floors. Laundromats. Gyms after closing. My first two nights in Salt Lake City I slept in Liberty Park, waiting on the cheapest Airbnb I could find to be ready, with my bag under my arm so nobody walked off with everything I owned.

I built my way out of that. More than a decade coaching people through hard changes taught me the same thing over and over. Almost nobody gets better at anything until they have a stable place to come home to.

So I got my license.

And I knew exactly what kind of agent I did not want to be, because I had already sat across from a few of them. When I was trying to buy a home myself, I left those meetings with almost nothing. No explanation of how the process actually worked. No real questions about my situation. No plan. Just a push toward their preferred lender, and frustration when I did not move on it fast enough.

Nobody ever told me why a pre-approval matters, or what it protects you from. They just wanted the box checked.

Understanding is the whole job

That gap is what I built my practice around. People deserve to understand the why behind the what. When someone knows the reason behind a step, they make better decisions and they stop being afraid. When they do not, they either freeze or they get taken advantage of.

I also train Jiu Jitsu, and that shapes how I see a transaction. A negotiation is a strategic exchange. There are positions, there is leverage, there is timing, and every move has a consequence. The person who understands the game controls it. The person who does not gets moved around by everybody else in the room.

A real estate contract is full of levers built specifically to protect you. Deadlines, contingencies, cancellation rights, inspection periods, appraisal protections. Most buyers and sellers never learn what those levers do until the moment they needed one and did not use it. My job is to make sure you know the board before we make the first move. Nobody I represent should ever look like an amateur in their own transaction.

And winning does not mean somebody has to lose badly. The best deals I have done ended with both sides signing something they felt good about. Anyone can strong-arm. Very few people can engineer an outcome where their client comes out ahead and the other side still shows up to closing willing to cooperate.

The part I care about most

The market has a habit of writing people off. If a file looks difficult, most agents move to an easier lead.

A researcher at the University of Utah called me in tears one afternoon. She was paid through a stipend, and she had already been turned down by her credit union, a national lender, a local lender, and several investors. She had the income. She just did not fit anyone's template. Everybody had told her she could not buy a home. I told her we would find the way. We found a builder's lender willing to underwrite the whole picture instead of one line on a form, and she closed on a townhome in American Fork, where she lives today with her family.

Not long after, I worked with a woman who had been at her job three months. Thin credit history, thin work history, rejection after rejection. Same answer from me. Same result for her.

And when Jared and his wife relocated here from Minnesota, they wanted a home listed at one million, one hundred ninety-five thousand dollars, sitting in active foreclosure after eleven months on the market, two prior agents and a failed auction. We closed it at nine hundred twenty thousand.

None of those were the easy version of the deal. That is exactly why I took them.

What this means for you

Your situation is probably nothing like mine, and that is fine. It does not change what I know, which is what it costs a person to not have a place that is theirs. I tell you the truth about a house, including the parts that cost me the commission. I run the numbers in front of you. And I do not let go until you are standing in your home with the keys in your hand.

I work with buyers and sellers in Utah County and Salt Lake County, with Real Broker, LLC and The Perry Group. If you want someone who will explain the entire process, and the reasoning behind every step of it, before asking you to commit to anything, that is the conversation I want to have.
